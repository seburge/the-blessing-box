Meet the Book

The Blessing Box

By Liv Fuller

Illustrated by Quincy Battieste

All Hugh wants for Christmas is a dog, but an out-of-sorts Santa has something else in mind—an idea his elves think is nuttier than a fruitcake.

When Hugh opens Santa’s unusual gift, the Blessing Box, his dream of a dog of his own crumbles like a Christmas cookie. But Hugh soon realizes the mystical box isn’t empty after all. It holds an invisible key called opportunity—one that could help him prove to his parents he’s ready for a dog.

From one Christmas to the next, Hugh, a sixth grader on the autism spectrum, works tirelessly to make his dream come true. But bullies, betrayal, and the challenge of standing up for himself may stand in the way.

Fortunately, Hugh’s mischievous best friend is determined to help—even if she lands in the doghouse trying. And if one extraordinary elf decides Hugh needs a little magic, not even Santa may be able to stop her.

When an unexpected accident forces Hugh to confront a life-or-death decision, he may have to sacrifice everything he has worked so hard for.

And if that happens, Santa’s elves fear that Santa may never come down a chimney again.

Come with Hugh on this magical adventure about friendship, the courage to be your true self, and the amazing discovery that sometimes the greatest gifts are the ones we give from the heart.

Meet the Author

Liv Fuller

Born in an idyllic small town in rural Wisconsin, I still remember the smell of the barn when I helped my dad treat cows with milk fever. The warmth of the old house after spending hours wading through snow drifts taller than I was. Climbing onto the hot summer roof to pelt my three younger siblings with water balloons. Wearing Green Bay Packers apparel to the little Lutheran church on the hill on crisp autumn Sundays. Playing little league and duck hunting with the boys—and beating them. And forging the strongest, longest friendships a tomboy could ask for.

I spent my early adult years in wild, wonderful Madison, Wisconsin. Mad-town, as it is called. Forty acres surrounded by reality. A place where minds stretched big, crimes cowered small, and crowds were quaint. I earned my journalism and law degrees from the University of Wisconsin-Madison, and I’ll forever have the fierce heart and drive of a Badger.

Over the past twenty-five years, metro Atlanta has become my second home. My career as an attorney and banker has paid the bills, but it’s my family that fills my heart.

I’m married to my soul mate and best friend. I’m a mama bear to two incredible children and two equally incredible bonus children. Along with our beloved pit bull rescues, they’re the loves of my life.

For all these fifty-some years, the artist in me has begged for attention. Over the past decade, I’ve finally given her what time I could, pouring my heart into writing The Blessing Box.

The character Hugh and many of the story’s events were inspired by the childhood experiences of one of our children, who was diagnosed with autism in early college. The Blessing Box is my love letter to that child and to everyone who has ever marched to the beat of their own drum, faced life’s challenges, and refused to give up.

To me, they are the real superheroes. Giving voice to ordinary people who dare to be different is both my passion and my privilege.

Always be you,
Liv

Quincy Battieste

Illustrator / Designer / Cartoonist

Quincy Battieste is an illustrator, designer, and cartoonist whose childhood love of drawing grew into a lifelong passion for visual storytelling. Originally from Anchorage, Alaska, he works in both traditional and digital mediums, creating imaginative artwork inspired by the absurdities of everyday life, pop culture, film, and literature. Ask for a Blessing Box

Give a child The Blessing Box Experience of their own

The Blessing Box Experience is a Christmas keepsake inspired by the children’s book The Blessing Box. Use it to give a child a goal to work toward, a reward to look forward to, and a reason to check in together along the way.

How it works

  1. Choose a challenge and a reward. Pick a goal that fits the child and a reward that you, or Santa, are ready to give when they reach it.
  2. Write a letter and tuck it inside the box. Explain what the child needs to do and what they’ll earn. Sign it yourself, or Santa will sign it if he is giving the child the box.
  3. Cheer them on. Check in as they work toward their goal, celebrate their progress, and follow through on the reward when they complete the challenge.

The Christmas wish that started it all

Hugh asks Santa for a dog. Santa gives him a box. What in the North Pole?!? The audacity!

Inside is a challenge. If Hugh earns and saves $500 and leaves it in his Blessing Box under the tree on Christmas Eve the following year, Santa will match it. That gives Hugh the $1,000 his dad says he needs for a dog of his own. Your child’s challenge can be as simple as saving for something they’d love or practicing a new skill. You choose the goal, the timeline, and the reward.

Be Creative

Choose a challenge that works for the child and for the family. A child could be challenged to volunteer, and for every five volunteer hours, the child gets to choose the restaurant the next time the family eats out. Or, the child could be challenged to make the bed every morning. Every day it’s done, the child gets 10 additional minutes of screen time. Although families and circumstances will vary (and likewise the Blessing Box Experiences will vary), Santa’s goal of teaching older children meaningful lessons such as working hard, reaping the rewards, and giving from the heart, are a common thread in the Christmas fabric.

What comes in The Blessing Box Experience

Each experience includes a sturdy cardboard Blessing Box, three sheets of silver writing paper, one sapphire-blue envelope, and one metallic-silver envelope seal. In Hugh’s story, Santa embosses his seal. It’s a little detail to know if your letter comes from the North Pole.

One Chapter at a Time, Together

Got ten minutes? That's about how long it takes to read a chapter of The Blessing Box aloud with the child you love—at bedtime, after dinner, or over a video call if you're miles apart. With twenty short chapters, you can even turn the story into a countdown, enjoying a chapter of magic each day until Christmas. Or, grab some cocoa, a blanket, and snuggle up with your favorite young reader if you have a little extra time together.

Before closing the book on a reading session, ask the child, "What do you think happens next?" in Hugh's quest for a dog. You'll also have chances to discuss being true to yourself, standing up for what matters, and giving from the heart.

And who knows? Perhaps a little of the magic you discover within these pages will find its way into your own home. After all, the most meaningful Christmas messages stay with us year round.

FAQ

What is The Blessing Box about?

The Blessing Box follows Hugh, a sixth grader on the autism spectrum who wants a dog for Christmas. Santa’s unusual gift sets him on a yearlong adventure of friendship, kindness, responsibility, and discovering the gifts we give from the heart. Read more about the book.

What genre is The Blessing Box?

Heartwarming, inspirational, Christian-based Christmas fantasy.

What age range is appropriate for The Blessing Box?

This story is perfect for young readers between ages 8 to 12, for the young at heart, and for those younger than 8 as a family read-along.

Who is the book for?

The book is written for late-elementary and middle-grade readers. Parents, grandparents, and other adults can also enjoy sharing the story with a young reader.

Does the story have a Christian perspective?

Yes. The Blessing Box is a Christian Christmas adventure that explores kindness, courage, and giving, with reminders that Jesus is the reason for the season.

Can we read it together during the holidays?

Yes. There are twenty chapters, so you can read one each night or settle in for a longer reading session.

Can I read a sample?

You can read Chapter Two, “A Stinky Afternoon”, on this website.

What inspired the story?

Hugh and many of the story's events were inspired by one of Liv's children. Read Liv’s story to learn more about the inspiration behind the book.

Are there illustrations?

Yes, The Blessing Box has at least one illustration per chapter. All interior illustrations are by Quincy Battieste. Given time constraints, we used an AI-generated cover based on Quincy's artwork. The second version of the book, with Quincy's cover illustration, will be published in the near future.

Who created the illustrations?

The interior illustrations are by Quincy Battieste and the AI-generated cover is based on his artwork.

What is the year of copyright?

The copyright is 2026, although the first version of the book has a typographical error and lists a copyright of 2006. Oops.

Are there resources for teachers, librarians, and reading groups?

Yes. The educator resources page includes discussion prompts and activities, plus a downloadable conversation starter based on the book description.

How can I ask about an interview, reading, or school visit?

Reviews and Testimonials

The Blessing Box is an absolute delight! Not only is it beautifully and expertly written, but the story itself is a fun-for-the-whole-family wholesome adventure filled with relatable peril, laugh-out-loud humor, and plenty of hope.

Every scene is bursting with lively, vivid descriptions that make the pages come alive. The writing is so wonderfully visual that the entire book feels cinematic, and I found myself imagining how beautifully it could translate to the big screen. Every character feels like a fully realized person with their own personality, quirks, and charm. In fact, the descriptions of the North Pole, Santa’s helpers, and all the elfecutives made me wish the place were real so I could visit! Everything feels so vibrant and magical. From Everly Grace’s hair, outfits, and magic to the wonderful details of Constance and the people who live there, the author’s imagination shines on every page. Even the gross-out moments (Hugh’s pants in the toilet, anyone?) are described with such skill that you can practically see them unfolding. The writing truly sparkles.

I also particularly appreciated the humor. The clever puns and playful turns of phrase had me laughing throughout, and I think this combination of humor and wonderfully descriptive storytelling is especially appealing to younger readers and adults alike. There’s so much personality woven into every chapter.

The plot and pacing are equally well done. Each chapter feels just the right length, with little cliffhangers that leave you eager to turn the page and discover what happens next. Even though the story spans an entire year, the pacing never feels rushed or skimps on the details. That’s not an easy balance to strike, but the author pulls it off beautifully.

All in all, The Blessing Box is a wonderfully imaginative, heartwarming adventure with memorable characters, delightful humor, and writing that transports you straight into its world. I genuinely hope someone turns this into a movie someday. I would be first in line to see it!

I wholeheartedly recommend picking up this book, especially if you're looking for something special to share with your children during the Christmas season. It's the kind of story that invites families to slow down, curl up together, and enjoy a little holiday magic. Whether you're reading it aloud as a family or letting an older child discover the adventure on their own, it's a wonderful addition to your Christmas reading traditions!
